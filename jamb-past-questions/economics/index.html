How does the JAMB Economics paper work?

Economics has 40 questions in the UTME and is scored out of 100, like each of your four subjects, for a total of 400. You write all four subjects in one 2-hour CBT sitting and can move between them freely, so the time you spend here comes out of your other subjects. No marks are taken away for wrong answers, so never leave a question blank.

JAMB Economics practice questions with answers

Answer each question before you open the explanation. Time yourself: aim for under a minute per question.

Question 1 basic concepts · easy

Chidi has ₦5,000. In order of preference he wants a textbook, a pair of jeans and a meal, and he can afford only one. If he buys the textbook, his opportunity cost is the

  1. textbook
  2. pair of jeans
  3. meal
  4. jeans and the meal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. pair of jeans

Opportunity cost is the next best alternative given up. The textbook was chosen, so the forgone second choice, the pair of jeans, is the opportunity cost. It is never the sum of all the alternatives forgone.

Question 2 demand and supply · medium

When the price of a good rises from ₦100 to ₦120, the quantity demanded falls from 50 units to 40 units. What is the price elasticity of demand?

  1. 0.5
  2. 1.0
  3. 1.5
  4. 2.0
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1.0

1. % change in quantity = (40 − 50) ÷ 50 × 100 = −20%.
2. % change in price = (120 − 100) ÷ 100 × 100 = 20%.
3. Elasticity = 20% ÷ 20% = 1.0 (ignoring the sign), so demand is unitary elastic.

Question 3 demand and supply · medium

Which of these would shift the demand curve for bread to the right?

  1. a fall in the price of bread
  2. a rise in consumers' incomes
  3. a rise in the price of flour
  4. better baking technology
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. a rise in consumers' incomes

For a normal good like bread, higher incomes mean people buy more at every price, so the whole demand curve shifts right. A fall in bread's own price only causes a movement along the curve, while flour prices and baking technology affect supply, not demand.

Question 4 market structures · easy

Which of the following is a feature of perfect competition?

  1. Firms are price takers.
  2. There is only one seller.
  3. Firms sell differentiated products.
  4. Entry into the industry is blocked.
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Firms are price takers.

Under perfect competition there are many small firms selling identical products, so no single firm can influence price: each is a price taker. Differentiated products describe monopolistic competition, and a single seller with blocked entry describes monopoly.

Question 5 money and banking · easy

Inflation caused by rising costs of production, such as higher fuel and transport costs, is called

  1. creeping inflation
  2. demand-pull inflation
  3. cost-push inflation
  4. galloping inflation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. cost-push inflation

When firms' costs rise, they pass them on as higher prices, which is cost-push inflation. Demand-pull inflation comes from too much spending chasing too few goods. Creeping and galloping describe how fast prices rise, not why.

Question 6 production · medium

A farm's total output is 10 bags with one worker, 24 bags with two workers and 33 bags with three workers. What is the marginal product of the third worker?

  1. 9 bags
  2. 11 bags
  3. 14 bags
  4. 33 bags
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 9 bags

1. Marginal product = change in total product from adding one worker.
2. 33 − 24 = 9 bags.
11 bags is the average product with three workers (33 ÷ 3), and 14 bags is the second worker's marginal product.

Question 7 national income · medium

A country's GDP is ₦500 billion and its net factor income from abroad is −₦20 billion. What is its GNP?

  1. ₦460 billion
  2. ₦480 billion
  3. ₦500 billion
  4. ₦520 billion
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. ₦480 billion

GNP = GDP + net factor income from abroad = 500 + (−20) = ₦480 billion. ₦520 billion comes from adding 20 instead of subtracting it; the negative sign means more income flows out of the country than comes in.

Question 8 money and banking · easy

Which of these is a function of the Central Bank of Nigeria but NOT of commercial banks?

  1. accepting deposits from the public
  2. granting loans to customers
  3. issuing the national currency
  4. keeping valuables in safe custody
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. issuing the national currency

Only the Central Bank of Nigeria issues the naira. Accepting deposits, lending to customers and safe custody of valuables are all everyday functions of commercial banks.

Which Economics topics come up most in JAMB?

These are the areas that appear again and again in JAMB Economics past questions. Check the full list in our JAMB syllabus guide.

  • Basic concepts: Scarcity, choice, scale of preference and opportunity cost, and economic systems.
  • Demand, supply and price: Determinants of demand and supply, shifts versus movements, elasticity calculations and price controls.
  • Production and costs: Factors of production, the law of diminishing returns, total, average and marginal product and cost.
  • Market structures: Perfect competition, monopoly, monopolistic competition and oligopoly.
  • National income: GDP, GNP and NNP, and the circular flow of income.
  • Money, banking and inflation: Functions of money, commercial banks and the Central Bank, and types of inflation.
  • Public finance and trade: Taxation, budgets and debt, comparative advantage, terms of trade and the balance of payments.

How to score high in JAMB Economics

  • Know the difference between a shift of the demand curve and a movement along it. JAMB tests it every year.
  • For elasticity and product calculations, write the formula first. The wrong options are the ones you get from dividing the wrong way round.
  • Learn the Nigerian context: the CBN's functions, ECOWAS and Nigeria's major exports appear often.
