How does the JAMB Mathematics paper work?

Mathematics has 40 questions in the UTME and is scored out of 100, like each of your four subjects, for a total of 400. You write all four subjects in one 2-hour CBT sitting and can move between them freely, so the time you spend here comes out of your other subjects. No marks are taken away for wrong answers, so never leave a question blank.

JAMB Mathematics practice questions with answers

Answer each question before you open the explanation. Time yourself: aim for under a minute per question.

Question 1 number bases · easy

Convert 1011₂ to a number in base ten.

  1. 9
  2. 11
  3. 13
  4. 22
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 11

1. Write out the place values: 1×2³ + 0×2² + 1×2¹ + 1×2⁰.
2. That is 8 + 0 + 2 + 1 = 11.
9 comes from dropping the last digit's value of 1 and 13 from reading the digits in the wrong order.

Question 2 indices and logarithms · medium

Evaluate 8^(2/3) × 4^(−1/2).

  1. 1/2
  2. 2
  3. 4
  4. 8
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 2

1. 8^(2/3) = (cube root of 8)² = 2² = 4.
2. 4^(−1/2) = 1 ÷ √4 = 1/2.
3. 4 × 1/2 = 2.
8 is what you get if you ignore the negative sign and multiply by 2 instead of dividing.

Question 3 indices and logarithms · easy

Evaluate log₂32 − log₂4.

  1. 3
  2. 5
  3. 8
  4. 28
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 3

1. log₂32 − log₂4 = log₂(32 ÷ 4) = log₂8.
2. 2³ = 8, so log₂8 = 3.
28 comes from subtracting the numbers (32 − 4) instead of dividing them, which the law of logarithms does not allow.

Question 4 algebra · easy

Find the roots of the equation x² − 5x + 6 = 0.

  1. −3 and −2
  2. −6 and 1
  3. 1 and 6
  4. 2 and 3
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 2 and 3

1. Look for two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to −5: −2 and −3.
2. So (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0, giving x = 2 or x = 3.
−3 and −2 are the numbers inside the brackets, not the roots; setting each bracket to zero changes their signs.

Question 5 sequences and series · medium

Find the 20th term of the arithmetic progression 3, 7, 11, …

  1. 75
  2. 79
  3. 80
  4. 83
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 79

1. First term a = 3, common difference d = 4.
2. nth term = a + (n − 1)d = 3 + 19 × 4 = 3 + 76 = 79.
83 comes from using n instead of n − 1, that is 3 + 20 × 4.

Question 6 statistics and probability · hard

A bag contains 4 red balls and 6 blue balls. Two balls are picked one after the other without replacement. What is the probability that both are red?

  1. 2/15
  2. 4/25
  3. 1/5
  4. 2/5
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 2/15

1. P(first red) = 4/10.
2. One red ball is gone, so P(second red) = 3/9.
3. P(both red) = 4/10 × 3/9 = 12/90 = 2/15.
4/25 is the answer with replacement (4/10 × 4/10); the question says without replacement.

Question 7 calculus · medium

If y = 3x² − 4x + 7, find dy/dx when x = 2.

  1. 4
  2. 8
  3. 11
  4. 12
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 8

1. dy/dx = 6x − 4.
2. At x = 2, dy/dx = 12 − 4 = 8.
11 is the value of y itself at x = 2, not the gradient.

Question 8 mensuration · medium

Find the volume of a cylinder of radius 7 cm and height 10 cm. (Take π = 22/7.)

  1. 440 cm³
  2. 770 cm³
  3. 1540 cm³
  4. 3080 cm³
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1540 cm³

1. Volume = πr²h = 22/7 × 7 × 7 × 10.
2. 22/7 × 49 = 154, and 154 × 10 = 1540 cm³.
440 cm³ is the curved surface area formula (2πrh), with the wrong unit.

Which Mathematics topics come up most in JAMB?

These are the areas that appear again and again in JAMB Mathematics past questions. Check the full list in our JAMB syllabus guide.

  • Number and numeration: Number bases, fractions and percentages, approximation, profit and loss, simple and compound interest, surds.
  • Indices and logarithms: The laws of indices and logarithms, and equations that use them. These are fast marks once the laws are automatic.
  • Algebra: Factorising, simultaneous and quadratic equations, change of subject, variation, inequalities, and the remainder and factor theorems.
  • Sequences and series: nth terms and sums of arithmetic and geometric progressions, including the sum to infinity.
  • Geometry and mensuration: Angles in polygons, circle theorems, areas, surface areas and volumes of solids, and loci.
  • Trigonometry and coordinate geometry: Trigonometric ratios, angles of elevation and depression, bearings, gradients and equations of lines.
  • Calculus: Differentiation, rates of change, maxima and minima, integration and areas under curves.
  • Statistics and probability: Mean, median and mode, charts, permutations and combinations, and the probability of combined events.

How to score high in JAMB Mathematics

  • Check options by substitution. If a question asks for the roots of an equation, putting each option back in is often faster than solving.
  • Watch the classic slips: n instead of n − 1 in sequences, 'with replacement' versus 'without', and forgetting to change Celsius to kelvin in mixed questions.
  • Skip any question that will take you more than two minutes, mark it, and come back. Every question is worth the same.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are in JAMB Mathematics?

JAMB Mathematics has 40 questions, scored out of 100. You share the 2-hour exam time across all four of your subjects, so aim for about 40 seconds to a minute per Maths question.

Can I use a calculator in JAMB?

The CBT system has an on-screen calculator. Practise with a basic calculator, and learn to do simple arithmetic in your head, because switching to the calculator for every step wastes time.

Which Maths topics come up most in JAMB?

Indices and logarithms, quadratic equations, sequences and series, mensuration, statistics and probability, and basic calculus appear almost every year.
