How does the JAMB Government paper work?

Government has 40 questions in the UTME and is scored out of 100, like each of your four subjects, for a total of 400. You write all four subjects in one 2-hour CBT sitting and can move between them freely, so the time you spend here comes out of your other subjects. No marks are taken away for wrong answers, so never leave a question blank.

JAMB Government practice questions with answers

Answer each question before you open the explanation. Time yourself: aim for under a minute per question.

Question 1 basic concepts · easy

The theory of separation of powers is most closely associated with

  1. A. V. Dicey
  2. Karl Marx
  3. Baron de Montesquieu
  4. Jean-Jacques Rousseau
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Baron de Montesquieu

Montesquieu set out the separation of powers in The Spirit of the Laws (1748), arguing that the legislative, executive and judicial powers should be held by different bodies to protect liberty. A. V. Dicey is associated with the rule of law, not the separation of powers.

Question 2 basic concepts · medium

Which of the following is NOT one of the principles of the rule of law as stated by A. V. Dicey?

  1. supremacy of the law
  2. equality before the law
  3. protection of individual rights
  4. concentration of power in the executive
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. concentration of power in the executive

Dicey's rule of law rests on the supremacy of the law, equality of all persons before the law, and the protection of individual rights through the courts. Concentrating power in the executive works against the rule of law rather than being part of it.

Question 3 federalism · easy

Which of these is an essential feature of a federal system of government?

  1. a flexible constitution
  2. a single legislature for the whole country
  3. constitutional division of powers between levels of government
  4. a ceremonial head of state
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. constitutional division of powers between levels of government

In a federation, the constitution divides powers between the central government and the component units (in Nigeria, the federal and state governments). Federal constitutions are usually rigid, not flexible, and a single legislature for the whole country is a feature of a unitary system.

Question 4 constitutional history · medium

Which constitution introduced regionalism into Nigeria?

  1. Clifford Constitution, 1922
  2. Richards Constitution, 1946
  3. Macpherson Constitution, 1951
  4. Lyttelton Constitution, 1954
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Richards Constitution, 1946

The Richards Constitution of 1946 divided Nigeria into three regions, East, West and North, each with a regional house of assembly. The Lyttelton Constitution of 1954 is tempting because it made Nigeria a federation, but the regions themselves came from the Richards Constitution.

Question 5 legislature · easy

A legislature made up of two chambers, like Nigeria's National Assembly, is described as

  1. unicameral
  2. bicameral
  3. tricameral
  4. bipartisan
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. bicameral

Bicameral means having two chambers. Nigeria's National Assembly has the Senate and the House of Representatives. Unicameral means one chamber, as in Nigerian state houses of assembly, and bipartisan describes cooperation between two parties, not chambers.

Question 6 pressure groups · medium

The main difference between a pressure group and a political party is that a pressure group

  1. has no members
  2. is registered by INEC
  3. does not seek to form a government
  4. operates only during elections
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. does not seek to form a government

A political party aims to win elections and control the government, while a pressure group tries to influence government policy without taking power itself. It is political parties, not pressure groups, that INEC registers.

Question 7 international organisations · easy

The Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) was established in

  1. 1960
  2. 1963
  3. 1975
  4. 2002
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1975

ECOWAS was founded by the Treaty of Lagos in May 1975. 1963 is when the Organisation of African Unity was formed, and 2002 is when the African Union replaced it.

Question 8 elections · easy

Universal adult suffrage means that

  1. only educated adults can vote
  2. only property owners can vote
  3. all qualified adult citizens can vote
  4. all adults must stand for election
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. all qualified adult citizens can vote

Universal adult suffrage gives every qualified adult citizen the right to vote, whatever their sex, wealth, education or religion. Limiting the vote to the educated or to property owners is a restricted franchise, the opposite idea.

Which Government topics come up most in JAMB?

These are the areas that appear again and again in JAMB Government past questions. Check the full list in our JAMB syllabus guide.

  • Elements of government: Power, authority, legitimacy, sovereignty, democracy, the rule of law and separation of powers.
  • Forms of government: Unitary, federal and confederal systems; presidential and parliamentary systems.
  • Constitutions: Types of constitution and Nigeria's constitutional history: Clifford, Richards, Macpherson, Lyttelton and the post-independence constitutions.
  • Arms of government: The structure and functions of the legislature, executive and judiciary.
  • Elections, parties and pressure groups: Franchise, electoral systems, INEC, and the functions of political parties and pressure groups.
  • Public administration: Local government, the civil service and public corporations.
  • Nigeria and the world: Nigeria's foreign policy and the UN, AU, ECOWAS, Commonwealth and OPEC.

How to score high in JAMB Government

  • Make a one-page timeline of Nigeria's constitutions with one key change for each. It answers several questions every year.
  • Learn which thinker goes with which idea: Montesquieu and separation of powers, Dicey and the rule of law.
  • Dates of international organisations are easy marks: OAU 1963, ECOWAS 1975, AU 2002.
