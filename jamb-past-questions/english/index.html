JAMB Use of English past questions and answers

Free JAMB Use of English practice questions with answers and full explanations, plus the topics that come up every year, how the 60-question paper works and how to stop losing easy marks.

Use of English is the only compulsory UTME subject and the longest paper: 60 questions, against 40 for every other subject, still marked out of 100. The marks that repeat every year come from synonyms and antonyms, sentence completion, concord, idioms, oral English (vowels, consonants and stress), comprehension passages and the set novel. How does the JAMB Use of English paper work?

Use of English has 60 questions in the UTME and is scored out of 100, like each of your four subjects, for a total of 400. You write all four subjects in one 2-hour CBT sitting and can move between them freely, so the time you spend here comes out of your other subjects. No marks are taken away for wrong answers, so never leave a question blank.

JAMB Use of English practice questions with answers

Answer each question before you open the explanation. Time yourself: aim for under a minute per question.

Question 1 synonyms and antonyms · easy

Choose the option nearest in meaning to the word in capitals: The manager's remarks were so CANDID that some of the staff were offended.

  1. frank
  2. rude
  3. careless
  4. clever
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. frank

Candid means open and honest, saying what you really think, so frank is the nearest meaning. Rude is tempting because candid remarks can offend, but candid describes honesty, not bad manners.

Question 2 synonyms and antonyms · medium

Choose the option opposite in meaning to the word in capitals: Adaeze is usually FRUGAL with her pocket money.

  1. careful
  2. extravagant
  3. secretive
  4. thrifty
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. extravagant

Frugal means spending as little as possible, so its opposite is extravagant, spending freely or wastefully. Thrifty and careful are close in meaning to frugal, which is exactly why JAMB puts them there as traps.

Question 3 lexis and structure · medium

Choose the option that best completes the sentence: Neither the principal nor the teachers ___ aware of the change.

  1. is
  2. was
  3. were
  4. has been
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. were

With 'neither … nor', the verb agrees with the subject closest to it. The nearer subject here is 'the teachers', which is plural, so the verb is were. Was would only be right if the sentence ended with a singular subject, as in 'neither the teachers nor the principal was'.

Question 4 lexis and structure · medium

Choose the correct question tag: Nobody called while I was out, ___?

  1. did he
  2. didn't they
  3. did they
  4. didn't he
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. did they

'Nobody' already makes the statement negative, so the tag must be positive. Indefinite pronouns like nobody and everyone take 'they' in the tag, which gives did they. Didn't they is the common slip of treating the statement as positive.

Question 5 idioms · easy

Choose the option that best explains the expression in capitals: After years of quarrelling, Emeka decided to BURY THE HATCHET with his brother.

  1. make peace
  2. keep a secret
  3. start a fight
  4. share their property
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. make peace

To bury the hatchet means to end a quarrel and make peace. The hatchet stands for the weapon of the old fight, and burying it means putting the fight away, not starting one.

Question 6 lexis and structure · medium

Choose the option that best completes the sentence: The students were congratulated ___ their excellent results.

  1. on
  2. for
  3. at
  4. with
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. on

In standard English you congratulate someone on an achievement, so on is correct. 'Congratulated for' is common in speech in Nigeria, which is why it is the tempting wrong option, but JAMB marks 'on' as correct.

Question 7 oral English · easy

Choose the word that has the same vowel sound as the letters in capitals: sEAt

  1. sit
  2. key
  3. set
  4. bread
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. key

The vowel in seat is the long sound /iː/. Key is pronounced /kiː/, with the same long vowel. Sit has the short /ɪ/, and set and bread both have /e/, even though bread is spelt with 'ea' like seat.

Question 8 oral English · hard

Which of these words is stressed on the second syllable?

  1. photograph
  2. photography
  3. camera
  4. elephant
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. photography

Photography is stressed pho-TO-gra-phy, on the second syllable. Photograph, camera and elephant are all stressed on the first syllable. Adding '-y' to photograph moves the stress, a pattern JAMB likes to test.

Which Use of English topics come up most in JAMB?

These are the areas that appear again and again in JAMB Use of English past questions. Check the full list in our JAMB syllabus guide.

  • Synonyms and antonyms: 'Nearest in meaning' and 'opposite in meaning' questions. The traps are words that look related but aren't, and options that are close in meaning to the word when you want the opposite.
  • Lexis and structure: Sentence completion, concord (subject–verb agreement), tenses, prepositions, question tags and reported speech.
  • Idioms and figurative expressions: What expressions like 'bury the hatchet' or 'a storm in a teacup' actually mean, and words that belong to particular fields (register).
  • Oral English: About ten questions on vowel and consonant sounds, words that rhyme, word stress and emphatic stress. Learn the sounds, not the spellings.
  • Comprehension and summary: Passages followed by questions on the main idea, facts, inference, tone and the meaning of words in context.
  • The set novel: Questions on the prescribed reading text. The Lekki Headmaster was the text for 2025 and 2026; JAMB hasn't named the 2027 text yet.

How to score high in JAMB Use of English

  • With 60 questions, English eats the most time. Do the lexis and oral questions quickly and leave comprehension passages for when you've settled.
  • For stress and vowel questions, say the words in your head the way a newscaster would, not the way you'd say them in a hurry.
  • Learn five new words a day with their opposites. Our synonyms and antonyms guide shows the method.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are in JAMB Use of English?

Use of English has 60 questions in the UTME, while each of your other three subjects has 40. It is still scored out of 100 like every other subject.

Is the JAMB novel part of Use of English?

Yes. Questions on the prescribed reading text appear in the Use of English paper. The Lekki Headmaster was the text for the 2025 and 2026 UTME, and JAMB has not yet named the 2027 text.

What is the hardest part of JAMB English?

Most candidates lose marks on oral English (stress and vowel sounds) and on antonyms, because the options are designed to look right. Both improve quickly with daily practice.
