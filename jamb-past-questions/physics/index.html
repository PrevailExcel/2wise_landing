How does the JAMB Physics paper work?

Physics has 40 questions in the UTME and is scored out of 100, like each of your four subjects, for a total of 400. You write all four subjects in one 2-hour CBT sitting and can move between them freely, so the time you spend here comes out of your other subjects. No marks are taken away for wrong answers, so never leave a question blank.

JAMB Physics practice questions with answers

Answer each question before you open the explanation. Time yourself: aim for under a minute per question.

Question 1 motion and forces · easy

A car starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at 2 m/s² for 5 s. How far does it travel in this time?

  1. 10 m
  2. 20 m
  3. 25 m
  4. 50 m
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 25 m

1. Use s = ut + ½at², with u = 0.
2. s = ½ × 2 × 5² = ½ × 2 × 25 = 25 m.
50 m comes from leaving out the ½.

Question 2 motion and forces · medium

A 2 kg ball moving at 3 m/s collides with a stationary 1 kg ball and they move off together. What is their common velocity?

  1. 1 m/s
  2. 1.5 m/s
  3. 2 m/s
  4. 3 m/s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 2 m/s

1. Momentum before = 2 × 3 + 1 × 0 = 6 kg m/s.
2. Momentum is conserved: (2 + 1) × v = 6, so v = 2 m/s.
1.5 m/s comes from wrongly averaging the two speeds.

Question 3 work, energy and power · easy

A man lifts a 50 kg load through a height of 4 m in 10 s. What power does he develop? (g = 10 m/s²)

  1. 20 W
  2. 200 W
  3. 500 W
  4. 2000 W
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 200 W

1. Work done = mgh = 50 × 10 × 4 = 2000 J.
2. Power = work ÷ time = 2000 ÷ 10 = 200 W.
2000 W is the work done in joules, not the power.

Question 4 current electricity · medium

Two 6 Ω resistors are connected in parallel across a 12 V battery of negligible internal resistance. What current flows from the battery?

  1. 1 A
  2. 2 A
  3. 4 A
  4. 6 A
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 4 A

1. Two equal resistors in parallel: R = 6 ÷ 2 = 3 Ω.
2. I = V ÷ R = 12 ÷ 3 = 4 A.
1 A is the answer for the resistors in series (12 ÷ 12).

Question 5 optics · medium

An object is placed 30 cm from a converging lens of focal length 10 cm. How far from the lens is the image formed?

  1. 7.5 cm
  2. 15 cm
  3. 20 cm
  4. 30 cm
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 15 cm

1. Lens formula: 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = 1/10 − 1/30.
2. 1/v = 3/30 − 1/30 = 2/30, so v = 15 cm.
7.5 cm comes from adding 1/10 and 1/30 instead of subtracting.

Question 6 heat · medium

How much heat is needed to raise the temperature of 2 kg of water from 20 °C to 70 °C? (Specific heat capacity of water = 4200 J/kg K)

  1. 42 kJ
  2. 210 kJ
  3. 420 kJ
  4. 588 kJ
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 420 kJ

1. Q = mcΔθ, with Δθ = 70 − 20 = 50 K.
2. Q = 2 × 4200 × 50 = 420 000 J = 420 kJ.
588 kJ comes from using the final temperature (70) instead of the change in temperature.

Question 7 waves · easy

A wave has a frequency of 50 Hz and a wavelength of 0.4 m. What is its speed?

  1. 0.008 m/s
  2. 12.5 m/s
  3. 20 m/s
  4. 125 m/s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 20 m/s

1. v = fλ = 50 × 0.4 = 20 m/s.
125 m/s comes from dividing frequency by wavelength instead of multiplying.

Question 8 radioactivity · medium

A radioactive sample has a mass of 80 g and a half-life of 3 days. What mass remains undecayed after 9 days?

  1. 10 g
  2. 20 g
  3. 26.7 g
  4. 40 g
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 10 g

1. 9 days ÷ 3 days = 3 half-lives.
2. 80 → 40 → 20 → 10 g.
26.7 g comes from dividing 80 by 3, but radioactive decay halves the mass each half-life; it does not split it into equal parts.

Which Physics topics come up most in JAMB?

These are the areas that appear again and again in JAMB Physics past questions. Check the full list in our JAMB syllabus guide.

  • Motion and forces: Equations of uniformly accelerated motion, velocity–time graphs, Newton's laws, momentum, projectiles and circular motion.
  • Work, energy, power and machines: Work done, kinetic and potential energy, power, and the mechanical advantage, velocity ratio and efficiency of machines.
  • Heat: Temperature scales, specific heat capacity, latent heat, expansivity and the gas laws.
  • Waves and sound: The wave equation v = fλ, properties of waves, resonance in pipes and strings.
  • Light: Reflection, refraction, refractive index and critical angle, the lens formula and optical instruments.
  • Electricity and magnetism: Ohm's law, series and parallel circuits, e.m.f. and internal resistance, electrical power, electromagnetic induction and transformers.
  • Modern physics: Atomic models, radioactivity and half-life, the photoelectric effect, and fission and fusion.

How to score high in JAMB Physics

  • Write the units next to every number. Many wrong options in JAMB Physics come from mixing cm and m, or °C and K.
  • Learn the 'trap' answers: forgetting the ½ in ½at², adding instead of subtracting in the lens formula, using total temperature instead of the change.
  • Draw a quick sketch for circuits and lens questions. Ten seconds of drawing saves a wrong answer.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are in JAMB Physics?

JAMB Physics has 40 questions, scored out of 100, taken alongside Use of English and two other subjects in a 2-hour CBT exam.

Is JAMB Physics hard?

Physics feels hard when you memorise formulas without practising them. Most JAMB Physics questions use one formula and simple numbers, so timed practice is what moves your score.

Which Physics topics come up most in JAMB?

Motion and momentum, current electricity, light, heat and radioactivity come up every year.
