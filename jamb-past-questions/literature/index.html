How does the JAMB Literature-in-English paper work?

Literature-in-English has 40 questions in the UTME and is scored out of 100, like each of your four subjects, for a total of 400. You write all four subjects in one 2-hour CBT sitting and can move between them freely, so the time you spend here comes out of your other subjects. No marks are taken away for wrong answers, so never leave a question blank.

JAMB Literature-in-English practice questions with answers

Answer each question before you open the explanation. Time yourself: aim for under a minute per question.

Question 1 literary devices · easy

Identify the figure of speech in the line: 'The sun smiled down on the tired farmers.'

  1. simile
  2. hyperbole
  3. metaphor
  4. personification
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. personification

Smiling is a human action, and here it is given to the sun, which makes this personification. It is not a simile because there is no 'like' or 'as', and nothing is exaggerated, so it is not hyperbole.

Question 2 literary devices · easy

'Bola bought big brown buns at the bus stop.' The sound device in this line is

  1. rhyme
  2. assonance
  3. alliteration
  4. onomatopoeia
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. alliteration

The repetition of the same consonant sound, /b/, at the start of nearby words is alliteration. Assonance is the repetition of vowel sounds, and onomatopoeia is a word that imitates a sound, such as 'buzz'.

Question 3 literary devices · medium

The expression 'a deafening silence' is an example of

  1. irony
  2. oxymoron
  3. antithesis
  4. euphemism
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. oxymoron

An oxymoron puts two contradictory words side by side, here 'deafening' and 'silence', for effect. Antithesis also involves contrast, but it sets opposing ideas against each other in balanced phrases or clauses rather than joining two words into one expression.

Question 4 poetry · easy

How many lines does a sonnet have?

  1. 10
  2. 12
  3. 14
  4. 16
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 14

A sonnet has 14 lines. The Shakespearean sonnet arranges them as three quatrains and a couplet, and the Petrarchan sonnet as an octave of eight lines followed by a sestet of six.

Question 5 prose · easy

A narrator who tells the story using 'I' and takes part in the events is a

  1. first-person narrator
  2. second-person narrator
  3. third-person narrator
  4. omniscient narrator
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. first-person narrator

A narrator who says 'I' and is a character in the story is a first-person narrator. An omniscient narrator stands outside the story and knows the thoughts of every character.

Question 6 drama · medium

In tragedy, the flaw in the hero's character that leads to his downfall is called

  1. catharsis
  2. hamartia
  3. peripeteia
  4. denouement
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. hamartia

Hamartia is Aristotle's term for the tragic flaw or error that brings about the hero's fall. Catharsis is the release of pity and fear the audience feels, and peripeteia is the sudden reversal of fortune itself.

Question 7 drama · medium

When the audience knows something important that a character on stage does not know, the technique is called

  1. satire
  2. suspense
  3. verbal irony
  4. dramatic irony
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. dramatic irony

Dramatic irony depends on the audience knowing more than the character. Verbal irony is different: a character says one thing but means the opposite.

Question 8 poetry · easy

A poem written to mourn the death of a person is

  1. an ode
  2. an epic
  3. a ballad
  4. an elegy
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. an elegy

An elegy is a poem of mourning or lament for the dead. An ode is a poem of praise, an epic is a long narrative poem about heroic deeds, and a ballad tells a story in short, song-like stanzas.

Which Literature-in-English topics come up most in JAMB?

These are the areas that appear again and again in JAMB Literature-in-English past questions. Check the full list in our JAMB syllabus guide.

  • Literary devices: Figures of speech such as simile, metaphor, personification, hyperbole and oxymoron, and sound devices such as alliteration and assonance.
  • Poetry: Poetic forms (sonnet, ode, elegy, ballad), and analysis of unseen poems: tone, mood and imagery.
  • Prose: Types of prose fiction, narrative techniques and points of view.
  • Drama: Types of drama, tragic terms like hamartia and catharsis, and dramatic techniques such as dramatic irony.
  • Unseen extracts: Short passages of prose, poetry or drama with questions on subject, technique and tone.
  • Prescribed texts: Drama, prose and poems on JAMB's list. Questions test plot, characters, themes and setting, so read each text, not just a summary.

How to score high in JAMB Literature-in-English

  • Learn device definitions with one example each, written in your own words.
  • For unseen poems, read the extract twice before looking at the questions, and answer only from the lines in front of you.
  • Read each prescribed text in full once, then revise with notes on characters, themes and key events.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are in JAMB Literature-in-English?

JAMB Literature-in-English has 40 questions, scored out of 100.

Are JAMB Literature questions only on the prescribed texts?

No. A large share test general literary devices, poetry, prose and drama terms, and unseen extracts. The prescribed texts are an important part, but not all of it.

Where can I find the JAMB Literature texts?

The prescribed texts are listed in the JAMB Literature-in-English syllabus on jamb.gov.ng. Check the list when you register, because texts change over time.
