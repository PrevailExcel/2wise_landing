How does the JAMB Biology paper work?

Biology has 40 questions in the UTME and is scored out of 100, like each of your four subjects, for a total of 400. You write all four subjects in one 2-hour CBT sitting and can move between them freely, so the time you spend here comes out of your other subjects. No marks are taken away for wrong answers, so never leave a question blank.

JAMB Biology practice questions with answers

Answer each question before you open the explanation. Time yourself: aim for under a minute per question.

Question 1 cells · easy

Which organelle is the main site of aerobic respiration in a cell?

  1. nucleus
  2. ribosome
  3. chloroplast
  4. mitochondrion
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. mitochondrion

The mitochondrion carries out the later stages of aerobic respiration and releases most of the cell's energy as ATP, which is why it is called the powerhouse of the cell. The chloroplast is where photosynthesis takes place, not respiration.

Question 2 cells · medium

What happens to a red blood cell placed in distilled water?

  1. It shrinks.
  2. It bursts.
  3. It becomes plasmolysed.
  4. It stays the same.
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. It bursts.

Distilled water is hypotonic to the cell, so water enters the cell by osmosis. A red blood cell has no cell wall to resist the pressure, so it swells and bursts (haemolysis). Plasmolysis happens to plant cells that lose water, not to animal cells that gain it.

Question 3 nutrition · medium

The oxygen released during photosynthesis comes from

  1. water
  2. glucose
  3. chlorophyll
  4. carbon(IV) oxide
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. water

In the light stage of photosynthesis, light energy splits water molecules (photolysis), and the oxygen given off comes from that water. Carbon(IV) oxide is the tempting answer because it contains oxygen, but its carbon and oxygen end up in the glucose made.

Question 4 transport · easy

Which chamber of the mammalian heart pumps oxygenated blood to the rest of the body?

  1. right atrium
  2. right ventricle
  3. left atrium
  4. left ventricle
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. left ventricle

The left ventricle pumps oxygenated blood into the aorta and round the body, which is why it has the thickest muscular wall. The right ventricle pumps deoxygenated blood only to the lungs.

Question 5 genetics · medium

In a plant, tallness (T) is dominant over shortness (t). If two heterozygous tall plants (Tt × Tt) are crossed, what percentage of the offspring are expected to be tall?

  1. 25%
  2. 50%
  3. 75%
  4. 100%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 75%

1. The cross Tt × Tt gives TT, Tt, Tt and tt in the ratio 1 : 2 : 1.
2. TT and Tt are both tall, so 3 out of 4 offspring are tall: 75%.
25% is the proportion of short (tt) plants.

Question 6 ecology · easy

In the food chain grass → grasshopper → lizard → hawk, the lizard is a

  1. producer
  2. primary consumer
  3. secondary consumer
  4. tertiary consumer
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. secondary consumer

The grass is the producer, the grasshopper that eats it is the primary consumer, and the lizard that eats the grasshopper is the secondary consumer. The hawk, at the next level, is the tertiary consumer.

Question 7 excretion · easy

What is the structural and functional unit of the mammalian kidney?

  1. neuron
  2. nephron
  3. alveolus
  4. villus
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. nephron

The nephron filters blood and forms urine, and each kidney contains about a million of them. Neuron is a common confusion because the words sound alike, but the neuron is the unit of the nervous system.

Question 8 classification · medium

Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of all living things?

  1. excretion
  2. respiration
  3. reproduction
  4. photosynthesis
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. photosynthesis

All living things respire, excrete and reproduce. Only green plants and some other organisms photosynthesise, and animals and fungi do not, so photosynthesis is not a characteristic of all living things.

Which Biology topics come up most in JAMB?

These are the areas that appear again and again in JAMB Biology past questions. Check the full list in our JAMB syllabus guide.

  • Variety of organisms: Characteristics of living things, classification into kingdoms, and the features of viruses, bacteria, fungi, plants and animals.
  • Cells: Organelles and their functions, diffusion, osmosis and active transport, and cell division.
  • Nutrition: Photosynthesis, mineral requirements of plants, food classes and digestion.
  • Transport, respiration and excretion: The mammalian heart and circulation, transport in plants, aerobic and anaerobic respiration, and the kidney.
  • Reproduction and coordination: Reproduction in plants and mammals, the nervous system, hormones and homeostasis.
  • Ecology: Food chains and webs, ecological factors, nutrient cycles, population studies, pollution and conservation.
  • Genetics and evolution: Mendel's laws and monohybrid crosses, sex determination, variation, and theories of evolution.

How to score high in JAMB Biology

  • Keep a short list of pairs you mix up: nephron and neuron, haemolysis and plasmolysis, xylem and phloem. Read it every morning.
  • For genetics, always draw the Punnett square. It takes ten seconds and stops ratio mistakes.
  • Read 'NOT' questions twice. JAMB Biology uses them often.
