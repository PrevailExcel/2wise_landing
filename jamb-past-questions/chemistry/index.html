How does the JAMB Chemistry paper work?

Chemistry has 40 questions in the UTME and is scored out of 100, like each of your four subjects, for a total of 400. You write all four subjects in one 2-hour CBT sitting and can move between them freely, so the time you spend here comes out of your other subjects. No marks are taken away for wrong answers, so never leave a question blank.

Question 1 the mole concept · easy

How many moles are in 11 g of carbon(IV) oxide, CO₂? (C = 12, O = 16)

  1. 0.25 mol
  2. 0.5 mol
  3. 2 mol
  4. 4 mol
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 0.25 mol

1. Molar mass of CO₂ = 12 + 2 × 16 = 44 g/mol.
2. Moles = mass ÷ molar mass = 11 ÷ 44 = 0.25 mol.
4 mol comes from dividing the molar mass by the mass (44 ÷ 11), the wrong way round.

Question 2 gas laws · medium

A gas occupies 300 cm³ at 27 °C. What will its volume be at 127 °C if the pressure stays constant?

  1. 225 cm³
  2. 400 cm³
  3. 600 cm³
  4. 1411 cm³
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 400 cm³

1. Charles' law needs kelvin: 27 °C = 300 K and 127 °C = 400 K.
2. V₂ = V₁ × T₂ ÷ T₁ = 300 × 400 ÷ 300 = 400 cm³.
1411 cm³ is what you get if you use the Celsius temperatures directly (300 × 127 ÷ 27).

Question 3 redox and electrolysis · medium

What is the oxidation number of manganese in KMnO₄?

  1. +2
  2. +4
  3. +6
  4. +7
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. +7

1. K is +1 and each O is −2, so four O atoms give −8.
2. The compound is neutral: +1 + Mn − 8 = 0, so Mn = +7.
+6 is a common slip from forgetting the +1 on potassium.

Question 4 periodic table · easy

An element X has atomic number 12. In which group and period of the periodic table is X?

  1. Group 2, Period 2
  2. Group 2, Period 3
  3. Group 3, Period 2
  4. Group 12, Period 3
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Group 2, Period 3

1. The electron configuration of 12 electrons is 2, 8, 2.
2. Two electrons in the outermost shell put X in Group 2, and three occupied shells put it in Period 3. (X is magnesium.)
Group 12 confuses the atomic number with the group number.

Question 5 acids, bases and salts · easy

A solution has a hydrogen ion concentration of 1 × 10⁻³ mol/dm³. What is its pH, and is it acidic or alkaline?

  1. pH 3, acidic
  2. pH 3, alkaline
  3. pH 11, acidic
  4. pH 11, alkaline
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. pH 3, acidic

1. pH = −log[H⁺] = −log(10⁻³) = 3.
2. Any pH below 7 is acidic.
pH 11 is the pOH-style slip of subtracting 3 from 14.

Question 6 organic chemistry · easy

What is the IUPAC name of CH₃CH₂CH₂OH?

  1. ethanol
  2. propanal
  3. propan-1-ol
  4. propanoic acid
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. propan-1-ol

The chain has three carbon atoms (prop-) with single bonds only (-an-) and an –OH group on the first carbon (-1-ol), so the name is propan-1-ol. Propanal would need a –CHO group and propanoic acid a –COOH group.

Question 7 equilibrium · medium

In the reaction N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), ΔH is negative. Which change increases the yield of ammonia at equilibrium?

  1. raising the temperature
  2. increasing the pressure
  3. adding a catalyst
  4. removing some nitrogen
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. increasing the pressure

There are 4 moles of gas on the left and 2 on the right. By Le Chatelier's principle, increasing the pressure shifts the equilibrium to the side with fewer gas moles, so more ammonia forms. A catalyst only helps equilibrium arrive faster; it does not change the yield.

Question 8 redox and electrolysis · hard

What quantity of electricity is needed to deposit one mole of copper from a solution of Cu²⁺ ions? (F = 96 500 C/mol)

  1. 48 250 C
  2. 96 500 C
  3. 193 000 C
  4. 289 500 C
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 193 000 C

1. Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu, so one mole of copper needs two moles of electrons.
2. Charge = 2 × 96 500 = 193 000 C.
96 500 C is enough for a metal with a +1 ion, such as silver, not copper.

Which Chemistry topics come up most in JAMB?

These are the areas that appear again and again in JAMB Chemistry past questions. Check the full list in our JAMB syllabus guide.

  • Stoichiometry and the mole: Moles, molar mass, reacting masses and volumes, concentration and titration.
  • Gases: Boyle's, Charles' and the general gas law, Graham's law of diffusion. Always convert to kelvin.
  • Atomic structure and the periodic table: Electron configuration, isotopes, groups and periods, and periodic trends.
  • Chemical bonding: Ionic, covalent, coordinate and metallic bonds, shapes of molecules and intermolecular forces.
  • Acids, bases and salts: pH, indicators, preparation of salts and titration.
  • Redox and electrolysis: Oxidation numbers, Faraday's laws and electrochemical cells.
  • Equilibrium, rates and energy: Le Chatelier's principle, factors affecting rate, and exothermic and endothermic reactions.
  • Organic chemistry: Naming hydrocarbons and functional groups, reactions of alkanes, alkenes and alcohols, and polymers.

How to score high in JAMB Chemistry

  • Learn the valencies and charges of common ions cold. They unlock oxidation numbers, formulas and electrolysis questions.
  • For gas law questions, convert to kelvin before anything else; the Celsius answer is always one of the options.
  • In organic chemistry, count the carbons first, then find the functional group. That's enough for most naming questions.
