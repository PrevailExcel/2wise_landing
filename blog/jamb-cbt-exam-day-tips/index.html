JAMB CBT exam-day tips: timing, strategy and what to bring

How to pace 180 questions in 2 hours, a two-pass strategy that protects your score, and the practical things to sort out before you get to the CBT centre.

PEPrevail EjimaduFounder of 2Wise · JAMB tutor at Eilit Hub, Aba · Updated 22 October 2026 · 3 min read

On JAMB day, aim for about 40 seconds per question: answer the easy ones first, flag the hard ones and come back, and never leave a question blank because wrong answers aren't penalised. Before the day, print your exam slip (JAMB advises one to two weeks ahead), confirm your centre, arrive early, and keep your fingers free of henna, since it can affect fingerprint verification.

Plenty of well-prepared candidates lose marks on exam day itself, through panic, poor pacing or a preventable hiccup at the centre. Here's how to make sure your preparation shows up in your score.

How should I pace 180 questions in 2 hours?

The average is about 40 seconds per question, but you won't spend it evenly. Some questions take 10 seconds; some take two minutes. That's why a two-pass strategy works:

  1. First pass: go through every subject answering what you know quickly. Flag anything that would take long.
  2. Second pass: return to the flagged questions with the time you saved.
  3. Last two minutes: make sure nothing is blank. JAMB doesn't penalise wrong answers.
Time checkpoints: after 30 minutes you should be about a quarter of the way through; after an hour, about halfway. If you're behind, speed up on your first pass and trust your flags.

Which subject should I start with?

There's no single right answer. Many students start with their strongest subject to build confidence and bank time. Whatever you choose, decide it during your practice mocks, not in the hall, and keep the same order every time so it's automatic.

What should I do in the weeks before the exam?

  • Print your exam slip as soon as printing opens (JAMB advises about one to two weeks before the exam). It shows your centre, date and time.
  • Visit your centre beforehand if you can, so you know how long the journey takes.
  • Keep your fingers free of henna or laali. Fingerprint verification is part of the process.
  • Sit at least two full timed mocks in the final fortnight, at the same time of day as your exam if possible.

What should I do on the morning of the exam?

  • Eat, and drink some water, before you leave
  • Arrive early: queues for verification can be long
  • Bring what your exam slip lists, and leave prohibited items (phones and other devices) at home or as instructed
  • When you sit down, check your name and subjects on the screen before you start

What if I panic in the hall?

It happens. Put your hands flat on the desk, take three slow breaths, and answer the next question you're sure of. One quick right answer resets your confidence. Practising under real timing beforehand is the best panic-proofing there is.

Frequently asked questions

How much time do I have per question in JAMB?

About 40 seconds on average: 2 hours (120 minutes) for 180 questions.

Should I guess in JAMB?

Yes. JAMB doesn't deduct marks for wrong answers, so a guess can only help. Never leave a question blank.

When should I print my JAMB exam slip?

JAMB opens slip printing about a week or two before the exam. Print it as soon as it's available so you know your exact centre, date and time.

Can henna stop me from writing JAMB?

It can cause problems. Fingerprint verification is part of the process, and henna or laali on the fingers has been reported to interfere with it. Keep your fingers clean before registration and the exam.
