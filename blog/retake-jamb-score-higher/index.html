Writing JAMB again? How to score much higher this time

A low JAMB score usually comes from a few fixable causes, not a lack of brains. Here's how to find out what went wrong and build a plan that changes the result.

PEPrevail EjimaduFounder of 2Wise · JAMB tutor at Eilit Hub, Aba · Updated 22 October 2026 · 3 min read

To score higher on a JAMB retake, don't start from scratch. First find out exactly where the marks went: sit a full timed mock to see which subjects and topics are weakest and whether timing was the problem. Then spend most of your study time on those weak topics, practise in the CBT format daily, and take a full mock every week until your score is consistently above your target.

If your JAMB result disappointed you, you're in large company, and it says far less about your intelligence than it feels like right now. Most low scores come from a handful of fixable causes. The work is finding yours.

Why do good students score low in JAMB?

  • No diagnosis. They studied hard, but spread evenly across topics they already knew and topics they didn't.
  • Timing. 180 questions in 2 hours is about 40 seconds each. Many candidates run out of time with dozens unanswered.
  • CBT unfamiliarity. The screen, navigator and scrolling all slow down someone who has only practised on paper.
  • Neglected English. Science students especially leave 15–25 marks in vocabulary and the novel.
  • Cramming. Long, last-minute sessions instead of steady daily practice.

Step 1: Find out where the marks went

Your JAMB result only gives four numbers. You need more detail than that. Sit a full timed mock, then look at:

  1. Which subject is lowest, and by how much
  2. Which topics inside each subject you get wrong most often
  3. Whether you finished, and how many questions you rushed at the end

Step 2: Spend your time where the marks are

Put most of your study time on your weakest topics. It feels uncomfortable, which is exactly why it works. Moving a weak topic from 30% to 70% adds far more to your total than polishing one you already score 85% in.

Step 3: Train for the clock and the screen

Do your daily practice in a CBT-style interface, and sit a full 2-hour mock every week. Use a two-pass approach: answer the easy questions first, flag the hard ones, come back, and never leave a blank, since JAMB doesn't penalise wrong answers. More in our CBT exam-day tips.

Step 4: Make your progress visible

Write your weekly mock score somewhere you can see it. Watching it climb is what keeps you going on the days motivation runs out, and it tells you, with evidence, when you're ready.

Frequently asked questions

Is it bad to write JAMB more than once?

No. Many students who are now in medicine, law and engineering wrote JAMB more than once. What matters is using the extra time well, not how many attempts it took.

What should I do differently when retaking JAMB?

Diagnose before you study. Use a full mock to find your weakest subjects and topics, then focus your time there, practise under time pressure, and track your mock scores weekly.

How long does it take to improve a JAMB score?

It depends on your starting point and consistency, but daily focused practice on weak topics, with weekly full mocks, typically shows clear improvement within a few weeks.

Should I change my course after a low JAMB score?

Not automatically. First check how far your score was from your course's real cut-off, and whether the gap is closable with a focused plan. Sometimes changing the institution is smarter than changing the course.
