JAMB past questions and answers: how to use them to actually score high

Does JAMB repeat past questions? Where can you get JAMB past questions for free, and how many should you do a day? Here's the method that turns past questions into a higher UTME score, subject by subject.

PEPrevail EjimaduSolutions Architect · GM, Eilit Hub · Updated 23 November 2026 · 7 min read

JAMB rarely repeats past questions word for word, but it tests the same topics and question patterns year after year. That's why JAMB past questions work, if you use them properly: answer them timed and on screen, mark yourself, sort every wrong answer by cause, fix that topic, then retest a few days later. Reading past questions with the answers showing teaches you almost nothing.

Every JAMB candidate has heard it: "Just read past questions." It's good advice, and it's also how many students spend months on JAMB past questions and still score below 200. The questions aren't the problem. The way most people use them is.

Does JAMB repeat past questions?

Not usually word for word. What JAMB repeats is topics and question types. The same syllabus topics come up every year, often asked in a slightly different way: a different number in a Mathematics question, a different word in a synonym question, a different element in a Chemistry question.

So the goal of practising JAMB past questions and answers isn't to memorise answers. It's to recognise the pattern so quickly that the new version of the question feels familiar. A student who has truly understood ten years of JAMB questions on indices won't be surprised by the eleventh.

Ignore "JAMB expo" and "runs". Anyone selling you this year's real questions is running a scam. JAMB has repeatedly warned candidates and parents against people offering "exam runs" or score manipulation, and candidates have been prosecuted for exam infractions. Real past questions plus real practice are the only shortcut.

Where can I get JAMB past questions?

  • Past question books for each subject, sold in most bookshops. Good for reading, but they don't time you or tell you where you're weak.
  • JAMB CBT practice apps, which put past and practice questions on a screen with a timer, the way the real UTME works. 2Wise has over 20,000 JAMB questions with explanations, and you can start free.
  • Your school or lesson centre, which often has compiled questions by topic.

Whatever you use, check that it covers your exact four subjects and the current Use of English set text. Questions on old JAMB novels won't come out again.

What's the wrong way to use JAMB past questions?

Reading the question, glancing at the options, then checking the answer. It feels productive because everything makes sense once you see the answer. But recognising a correct answer and producing it yourself, under time pressure, are different skills. The UTME tests the second one. (We explain the science behind this in why six hours of reading can lose to two hours of study.)

Other common mistakes:

  • Memorising the answer letter ("number 14 is C") instead of the reason
  • Only practising topics you already like
  • Never doing questions under a timer, then running out of time in the hall
  • Doing hundreds of questions without ever reviewing the wrong ones

How should you use JAMB past questions? The 5-step method

  1. Attempt them timed. JAMB gives you 2 hours for 180 questions, which is 40 seconds per question. Practise at that pace.
  2. Mark yourself honestly. Every unanswered question is a wrong answer.
  3. Sort every wrong answer by cause. Did you not know the topic? Misread the question? Make a careless calculation slip? Run out of time? Each cause has a different fix.
  4. Fix the topic, not the question. If you missed a question on variation, study variation, then do ten more variation questions.
  5. Retest after 2–3 days. Come back to the questions you got wrong. If you get them right without help, that topic is fixed.

JAMB past questions by subject: what keeps coming up

These are the areas we see again and again in JAMB past questions. Use them to decide where your practice time goes first. Always check against the official JAMB syllabus for your subjects.

JAMB Use of English past questions

Use of English has 60 questions, more than any other subject. The repeat areas are synonyms and antonyms (nearest and opposite in meaning), sentence completion and grammar (concord, tenses, prepositions, question tags), idioms, oral forms (vowel and consonant sounds, rhymes, word stress, emphatic stress), comprehension passages and questions on the set novel. Vocabulary questions are some of the most learnable marks in the whole exam, see JAMB synonyms and antonyms. Try free JAMB Use of English practice questions with answers.

JAMB Mathematics past questions

Expect number bases, fractions and percentages, indices, logarithms and surds, sets, quadratic equations, variation, sequences and series, inequalities, mensuration (areas and volumes), circle theorems, trigonometry, differentiation and integration, and statistics (mean, median, mode, range) with probability. Mathematics past questions reward speed: learn the shortcuts and check options by substitution when algebra would take too long. Try free JAMB Mathematics practice questions with answers.

JAMB Physics past questions

Motion and equations of motion, work, energy and power, heat and gas laws, waves and sound, light (reflection, refraction, lenses and mirrors), current electricity and circuits, electromagnetic induction, and elementary modern physics come up every year. Most Physics questions are a formula plus careful units, so practise until choosing the right formula is automatic. Try free JAMB Physics practice questions with answers.

JAMB Chemistry past questions

The mole concept and stoichiometry, atomic structure and the periodic table, chemical bonding, gas laws, acids, bases and salts, oxidation and reduction, electrolysis, rates of reaction and chemical equilibrium, metals and non-metals, and organic chemistry (hydrocarbons and functional groups) are the steady repeats. Try free JAMB Chemistry practice questions with answers.

JAMB Biology past questions

Classification of living things, cell structure and organisation, nutrition, transport, respiration and excretion, reproduction, ecology (ecosystems, food chains, adaptations), genetics and variation, and evolution. Biology questions often hinge on one precise term, so keep a list of definitions you keep mixing up. Try free JAMB Biology practice questions with answers.

Arts and commercial subjects

In Government, the repeat areas include arms of government, constitutions, federalism, the electoral process and Nigerian political history. In Economics, demand and supply, elasticity, market structures, national income, money and inflation. In Literature-in-English, literary devices, poetry appreciation and the prescribed texts. In CRS, IRS, Commerce and Accounts, the same principle applies: find the topics that repeat, then drill them.

How many JAMB past questions should I do a day?

Quality beats quantity, but you need volume too. A sensible daily target is 40–60 questions on your weakest topics, with every wrong answer reviewed, plus one full 180-question mock a week once you're within ten weeks of the exam. That's roughly 300–400 reviewed questions a week, which adds up to several thousand before the UTME.

Frequently asked questions

Does JAMB repeat past questions?

JAMB rarely repeats questions word for word, but it repeats the same topics and question types every year. Practising past questions helps you recognise those patterns, so new versions of familiar questions feel easy.

Where can I get JAMB past questions and answers for free?

Many CBT practice apps and websites offer free JAMB past questions. 2Wise lets you start free with full JAMB mocks and explanations across all subjects. Avoid anyone selling “expo” or this year's questions; that is always a scam.

How many years of JAMB past questions should I study?

Ten years of past questions per subject is a good target, but how you use them matters more than how many you see. Answer them timed, review every wrong answer and retest your weak topics.

Are JAMB past questions enough to pass?

Past questions are the best practice material, but they work best alongside the JAMB syllabus and your textbook. Use the syllabus to see every topic, past questions to see how JAMB asks them, and your textbook to fix gaps.

How long do I have per question in JAMB?

The UTME gives you 2 hours for 180 questions, which works out to 40 seconds per question on average. Practise past questions at that pace so the real exam doesn't rush you.
