JAMB aggregate score calculator

Universities don't admit on your JAMB score alone. They combine it with your Post-UTME score, your O'level grades, or both. Use this free calculator to work out your aggregate, then see what it means for your admission chances.

PEPrevail EjimaduFounder of 2Wise · JAMB tutor at Eilit Hub, Aba · Updated 27 November 2026

Most Nigerian universities convert your UTME score to 50% by dividing it by 8 (400 ÷ 8 = 50). The other 50% comes from either your Post-UTME score divided by 2 (if it's marked out of 100) or your O'level points (commonly A1 = 10 down to C6 = 5 across five subjects). Add the two halves to get your aggregate out of 100. Schools use different formulas, so always confirm your school's own one.

How is the JAMB aggregate score calculated?

There's no single national formula. Each university publishes its own, and some change it from year to year. But most use one of two patterns:

FormulaHow it worksExample
UTME + Post-UTMEUTME ÷ 8 (worth 50%) + Post-UTME ÷ 2 (worth 50%, if marked out of 100)280 in UTME and 65 in Post-UTME: 35 + 32.5 = 67.5%
UTME + O'levelUTME ÷ 8 (50%) + O'level points from five relevant subjects (50%)290 in UTME with four A1s and one B2: 36.25 + 49 = 85.25%

The O'level grading in this calculator (A1 = 10, B2 = 9, B3 = 8, C4 = 7, C5 = 6, C6 = 5) is one common scale. Others are in use: some schools score grades out of 4 or 6 and scale them, and some give a bonus for results from one sitting. Some schools combine all three: UTME, Post-UTME and O'level. Treat this calculator's result as a guide, and check your university's admissions page for the exact formula.

What aggregate do I need for admission?

It depends on your course and your school. Competitive courses like Medicine, Law, Nursing and Engineering usually need much higher aggregates than the minimum, and departmental cut-offs are set after each year's screening. Read JAMB cut-off marks: what you really need for how to estimate your real target.

Which part of the aggregate can you still change?

If you haven't written the UTME yet, your JAMB score is the biggest lever. Every 8 marks in the UTME is a full point on your aggregate. Moving from 240 to 280 adds 5 points, often the difference between getting your course and not. That's why the most useful thing to do before the exam is to find and fix your weak topics.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my JAMB aggregate?

For most universities, divide your UTME score by 8 and add your Post-UTME score divided by 2 (if the Post-UTME is out of 100). Some schools use O'level points instead of, or as well as, the Post-UTME. Always check your school's own formula.

Why is the JAMB score divided by 8?

Dividing the UTME score (out of 400) by 8 converts it to a score out of 50, so it makes up half of an aggregate out of 100.

What is a good aggregate score?

It depends on the course and school. For competitive courses at popular universities, successful candidates often have aggregates well above 70%, while less competitive courses admit lower. Check your department's past cut-offs.

Does every university use the same aggregate formula?

No. Each university sets its own formula, and some change it from year to year. This calculator covers the two most common patterns.
